Bio & Statement

Rafael Yaluff 1983, Santiago de Chile

Bio

Rafael Yaluff is a visual artist whose recent practice focuses on painting, drawing, and printmaking as part of a single creative flow that unfolds in the space between the abstract and the figurative. His images are born from intimate and immediate impulses, and over time are transformed through a process of semantic accumulation that reveals hidden layers of memory, language, and affect. Working from his studio in Berlin—often with Lo-fi and old-school Hip Hop as both rhythm and atmosphere—Yaluff develops visual narratives where emotional tension and formal economy coexist. This process has led him to what he calls "autistic expressionism": a method rooted in deep concentration, sensory precision, and a sustained attentiveness to complexity. His practice explores themes such as social behavior, neurodivergence, and the poetics of uncertainty, embracing contradiction not as conflict but as a productive force. Through this, he seeks to create a visual language where urgency and contemplation, structure and chaos, can coexist in dynamic balance.

Statement

Autistic Existentialism - Berlin, October 2026 I've been thinking about Andy Warhol’s mid 60’s period: Car crashes, suicides, race riots; Heavy stuff, but regardless of that, those paintings dont seem to pass judgement about it - Things are just there. Flat, direct, objective. Looking back from our point in history, that kind of detached neutrality seems impossible now. In this post-information era, no image arrives innocent. Everything is already filtered and ideologically loaded. Still, despite all this, i think painting can keep trying to clear space for another kind of seeing. Maybe as a way to hold complexity without explaining or simplifying it. At this stage i work from found images mostly from social media and reinterpret them in monochrome. These are images that usually disappear in the endless feed and my idea is to bring them into another pace and another scale where they can stay longer and absorb more meaning. My process starts instinctively and then moves into a more reflective stage. Sometimes i add visual symbols and references from poetry, philosophy, and psychology trying to shape a language where emotional intensity and conceptual clarity can coexist. As someone who identifies as neurodivergent, i’m interested in how autism and social behavior can be approached as existential and artistic material not as pathology. In this context autism appears to me less as diagnosis and more as method: as a way of perceiving details, sustaining attention, and resisting normalization. I call this approach autistic existentialism not as theory, but as practice. As a way to navigate uncertainty and still make meaning. In this frame painting becomes less about representation and more about perception itself, about how we construct reality from fragments. I don't aim to offer answers, i just try to hold contradictions in place and open up new possibilities for interpretation. This is where i stand now.
