Bio

Rafael Yaluff (Santiago, Chile, 1983) MFA in Studio Art from Hunter College, New York (2023) is a visual artist whose recent practice focuses on painting, drawing, and printmaking as part of a single creative flow that unfolds in the space between the abstract and the figurative. He began his artistic career painting landscapes en plein air in Chilean Patagonia, exploring the relationship between phenomenology and Impressionism. Over time, his practice evolved toward a deconstruction of the language of painting, informed by Neo-Expressionism, Pop, and Conceptual Art. His works, initially appearing abstract in their formal language, gradually reveal internal narratives constructed through humor and brutal honesty. Rafael Yaluff has participated in international exhibitions and residencies across Latin America, Europe, Canada, and the United States. These include The Immigrant Artist Biennial (USA), Chilean Connection (Germany), Prayers (Canada), Karaoke (Portugal), Premio MAVI (Chile), and representing Chile at the Pan American Games in Toronto. His work has been exhibited at institutions including Kunsthal Charlottenborg (Denmark) and is held in collections in Chile, the United States, Canada, and Portugal. He currently lives and works in Berlin.

Statement

Passive Existentialism I've been thinking about the mid 60’s photo based period of Andy Warhol, car crashes, electric chairs, plane crashes, suicides, race riots: regardless of the subject matter, the images are transported from the newspaper to the canvass: Here's an electric chair, here's a car accident, over and over, what do you think? This kind of operation struck me as similar to the Duchamp ready made’s, also transportations where things are brought to exist. What has been in the back of my mind about this artworks its their kind of passive existentialism: There was the urgency in their presence but not accompanied by judgment, things were displayed in a sort of passive voice. In today's world where at times everything seems it's about to blow up (just like in the Weimar Republic - a period of the arts that don't cease to inspire me) we often judge more than contemplate what we receive from the world, and there are plenty of reasons to do so, like our political, sexual, ecological conscience. I think that sometimes this mandates, the reactions and awareness we strive to have in our average consumer, digital personae life, fills us up with opinions and values, whether they come from us or from the vast landfill of judgment around us, fullness, regardless the stuffing, asserts something into me and that brings a short comfort, a satisfaction, and then this fullness grounds us. Therefore I try to keep judgment in brackets, and loose myself into the narrative. I work in an abstract way, and my work often gives the appearance of abstraction, but it isn't, rather it is a king of impressionism, where like an Impressionist decomposes the landscape into prime elements. I draw and try not to assert a judgement, but as a mentioned in the beginning of the text, to look at things, to lay them out, mostly things about the human condition that, like a lost bullet, hit me in the void, like characters in a movie. Drama, and Punk often flesh up my work. where I make drawings, paintings and sculptures, to look at situations. Especially the situations that resist an accurate description, using abstract techniques to address the narrative.
