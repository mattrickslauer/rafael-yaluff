Rafael Yaluff 1983, Santiago de Chile

Bio

Rafael Yaluff is a visual artist whose recent practice focuses on painting, drawing, and printmaking as part of a single creative flow that unfolds in the space between the abstract and the figurative. · He began his artistic career painting landscapes en plein air in Chilean Patagonia, exploring the relationship between phenomenology and Impressionism. Over time, his practice evolved toward a deconstruction of the language of painting, informed by Expressionism, Pop, and Conceptual Art. His works, initially appearing abstract in their formal language, gradually reveal internal narratives constructed through humor and drama. · Rafael Yaluff has participated in international exhibitions and residencies across Latin America, Europe, Canada, and the United States. These include The Immigrant Artist Biennial (USA), Chilean Connection (Germany), Prayers (Canada), Karaoke (Portugal), Premio MAVI (Chile), and representing Chile at the Pan American Games in Toronto. His work has been exhibited at institutions including Kunsthal Charlottenborg (Denmark) and is held in collections in Chile, the United States, Canada, and Portugal. · He currently lives and works in Berlin.

Statement

Passive Existentialism - Berlin, October 2026 I've been thinking about the mid 60’s photo based period of Andy Warhol, car and plane crashes, electric chairs, suicides, race riots: regardless of the subject matter, of which our time has its own fair share. The images in those paintings are transported from news to the canvass - Andy presents them: Here's an electric chair, here's someone trapped inside a wrecked car, what do you think?. This kind of operation struck me as similar to the Duchamp ready made’s, also transportations where things are brought to exist, as they are, not part of a narratives. This methodology has been in the back of my mind, I think of it as a kind of kind of passive existentialism: There's urgency in their presence but not accompanied by judgment, things are uttered in a passive voice. · In today's Berlin I have time, even though sometimes it seems as if everything is about to blow up (just like in the Weimar Republic - a period of the arts that don't cease to inspire me) In this city I can look at things. We are always judging, and the higher the pressure, the more difficult it gets to contemplate what we receive from the world. There are plenty of reasons to judge cultural expressions, political, sexual, ecological, to name a few. I think that sometimes this mandates, the reactions and awareness we strive to have, does not only guide us, but also fills us up with opinions and values, and its the filling up that I find most problematic, whether the stuffing comes from us or from the vast landfill of judgment around us, fullness asserts something into us, it brings both heaves and comfort, a satisfaction, and then this fullness grounds us. · But what of a work that grounds us while keeping judgment in brackets? I feel that some aspects of abstraction are useful for this purpose. I work in an abstract way, and my work often gives the appearance of abstraction, but it is only abstract as an impressionist painting is somewhat abstract, by decomposing the image into its prime elements. I also attempt to decompose narrative situations into its prime elements and try not to use them to assert a judgement about something, to make a point, but to show something. like characters in a movie. This is why Drama and often Punk flesh up my work, both ways to experience elsewhere here. I make drawings, paintings and sculptures, I look at situations, especially situations that resist an accurate description, climaxes, paths that allow me to venture out of what I am, work is that extasis, that why some days I'm not Rafael, but Xoltz Popov, a russian, Bein myself I could only preach, and I don't want to do that.
